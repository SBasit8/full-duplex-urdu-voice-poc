DATA Complexity and Challenges:

A real conversation recording — whether it is a phone call, a podcast, or a TV interview — almost always arrives as a single mono or blended stereo file where both voices are physically mixed together in the audio waveform. You cannot simply cut Speaker A out of this because the two voices are overlapping pressure waves combined into the same signal. This is like mixing red and blue paint — you cannot just scoop the red out again.

AUDIO Seperation PROBLEMS:
To turn this into the separated stereo format training requires, you need to solve three problems in sequence. 

First, diarization — figuring out which time segments belong to which speaker. Tools like PyAnnote-3.1 do this by analysing speaker embedding differences across time windows. But when two people talk simultaneously, diarization has to pick one or estimate, and it gets this wrong frequently — especially in Urdu where code-switching and backchannels are constant.

Second, separation — once you know who spoke when, you silence the segments that belong to the other speaker on each channel, creating an imperfect separated stereo file. The "imperfect" part matters: there is residual bleed-through from the other speaker, quiet artifacts, and missed speech at boundaries.

Third, transcription — you need word-level timestamps for the training pipeline, which means running a forced-alignment ASR model on each channel separately, and Urdu ASR is still 15–27% WER even on clean speech.

PersonaPlex's actual data strategy is more nuanced than "100% synthetic." The published paper describes ~2,250 hours of synthetic dialogue (generated by Qwen3-32B/GPT-OSS-120B, synthesized via Chatterbox/Dia TTS). The synthetic data serves a complementary role: it provides task diversity, domain coverage, and volume, while real data provides the conversational naturalness properties that TTS fundamentally cannot simulate — overlapping speech, contextually appropriate backchannels, natural disfluencies, and emotional prosody variation




Single-speaker TTS had a clear problem (convert text to one voice), abundant training data (professional voice recordings), and a well-understood output format (one waveform). Multi-stream TTS has a harder problem (generate two contextually coupled voices simultaneously), almost no training data in the required format anywhere in the world, and requires a new codec and architecture that nobody had built before Kyutai. Kyutai spent years building all three pieces from scratch — the Mimi codec, the DSM architecture, and the 170-hour recording corpus — before the system could exist at all. That is why it is the only one that currently exists at meaningful quality.
